What happens when the gateway stays closed?

We are told a university degree is the golden gateway to a good life. Chris Kubai’s door never opened.

Growing up in Bushitsiula village in Kakamega County, Chris began again: from waiter and park attendant to security guard and gardener, then messenger to administrator at the Colombian Embassy. The road went on through the Danish Embassy, the British High Commission, the Embassy of Ireland, a UN mission in Goma and the Gates Foundation, until he founded his own company.

This memoir is a celebration of resilience and a tribute to the power of self-education. It shows how determination can carve out success in the most unexpected ways.

Pro­logue

Life, as I have come to understand it, does not always adhere to the neat, predictable scripts that society seems so eager to hand us.

Among these, one of the most entrenched beliefs is that a university degree stands as the golden gateway to a fulfilling and prosperous life.

Yet, what happens when that gateway remains firmly closed?

What becomes of dreams deferred and paths uncharted?

This memoir is my answer to those questions.

About the author

Chris Kubai

Chris Kubai is a Kenyan professional, project manager and global citizen with a passion for people, development, and creating opportunities. He was born in Bushitsiula village, Kakamega, in Western Kenya.

With over fifteen years of experience in programme operations, administration, liaison and project management, he has worked across different sectors and countries. He is the Founder and CEO of Insign Linkglobal Connect, which enables individuals and businesses to connect, collaborate and thrive.
